Column 2: Mainstream Jazz Genres & Timeline

Timeline of Mainstream Jazz Genres

From Ragtime to Fusion, trace a century of jazz evolution. Dates indicate the primary era of emergence; styles overlapped and cross-pollinated rather than strictly replacing one another.

  1. Late 19th C.
    —Early 20th C.
    Ragtime & Early Blues Syncopated piano and blue notes laid the rhythmic and harmonic foundation of jazz.
  2. 1910s—1920s
    New Orleans Traditional / Dixieland Collective polyphonic improvisation emerged, gradually giving rise to the soloists.
  3. 1930s—1940s
    Big Band Swing Big bands and infectious swing grooves made jazz the mainstream pop music of America.
  4. Mid-1940s
    Bebop Small combos, blistering tempos, and complex harmonies transformed jazz into fine listening art.
  5. Late 1940s—1950s
    Cool Jazz Restrained tones and sophisticated arrangements emphasizing space, lyricism, and subtlety.
  6. Mid-1950s—1960s
    Hard Bop Reconnected jazz to blues, gospel, and soul roots with driving grooves and fiery expression.
  7. Late 1950s—1960s
    Modal Jazz Replaced rapid chord changes with modal scales, opening vast harmonic space for improvisation.
  8. Late 1960s—1970s
    Jazz Fusion Blended rock, funk, and electric instruments to expand jazz's sonic frontier.

1. Roots Jazz: Ragtime & Blues

Late 19th ~ Early 20th C. · Syncopated Piano · Mississippi Delta Blues
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. The Entertainer — Scott Joplin Essential Track
  2. Maple Leaf Rag — Scott Joplin
  3. St. Louis Blues — Bessie Smith
The roots of jazz lie in African American spirituals, work songs, and rural blues of the late 19th century. Ragtime, pioneered by Scott Joplin, introduced sophisticated syncopated rhythms on piano, while Delta blues contributed the soulful expressive bending of blue notes. Together, they laid the bedrock for all jazz that followed.
Listen on YouTube (Scott Joplin - The Entertainer) Wikipedia Article

2. New Orleans Traditional / Dixieland (New Orleans Traditional Jazz & Dixieland)

1910s ~ 1920s · Collective Polyphony · Marching Brass & Soloists
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. West End Blues — Louis Armstrong Essential Track
  2. Dipper Mouth Blues — King Oliver
  3. Livery Stable Blues — Original Dixieland Jazz Band
Emerging in early 20th-century New Orleans, this foundational style blended marching brass bands, Creole melodies, and French quadrilles into polyphonic collective improvisation. Louis Armstrong’s dazzling virtuosity soon transformed jazz from an ensemble novelty into a profoundly expressive soloist's art form.
Listen on YouTube (Louis Armstrong - West End Blues 1928) Wikipedia Article

3. 大乐队摇摆乐时代 (Swing & The Big Band Era)

1930s ~ 1940s · 十余人大编制齐奏 · 华丽配器与四四拍跳动摇摆
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. Sing, Sing, Sing — Benny Goodman Essential Track
  2. Take the A Train — Duke Ellington
  3. One O’Clock Jump — Count Basie
Dominating the 1930s and '40s, the Swing Era saw jazz become mainstream popular music. Led by legends like Benny Goodman, Duke Ellington, and Count Basie, large 15-to-18-piece big bands energized dancehalls with swinging 4/4 walking basslines, intricate sectional call-and-response horn arrangements, and jubilant energy.
Listen on YouTube (Benny Goodman - Sing, Sing, Sing 1938) Wikipedia Article

4. 比波普 / 咆哮乐 (Bebop)

Mid-1940s · 极速即兴与延伸和弦 · 爵士乐晋身严肃艺术之转折点
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. Hot House — Charlie Parker & Dizzy Gillespie Essential Track
  2. Donna Lee — Charlie Parker
  3. ’Round Midnight — Thelonious Monk
Developed in the mid-1940s by Charlie Parker, Dizzy Gillespie, and Thelonious Monk, bebop was an intellectual revolt against swing commercialism. Featuring lightning-fast tempos, dissonant harmonic substitutions, and asymmetrical phrasing, bebop fundamentally transformed jazz into a modern, sophisticated listening music.
Listen on YouTube (Parker & Gillespie - Hot House) Wikipedia Article

5. 冷爵士 / 酷派爵士 (Cool Jazz)

1940s 末 ~ 1950s · 典雅克制音色 · 古典对位与内省诗意
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. Take Five — The Dave Brubeck Quartet Essential Track
  2. Boplicity — Miles Davis
  3. My Funny Valentine — Chet Baker
Emerging in the late 1940s and 1950s—most notably with Miles Davis's Birth of the Cool—cool jazz favored subtle dynamics, restrained vibrato, relaxed tempos, and classical counterpoint over bebop's frenetic heat. Celebrated figures like Dave Brubeck and Chet Baker brought an elegant, understated poise to the genre.
Listen on YouTube (The Dave Brubeck Quartet - Take Five) Wikipedia Article

6. 硬波普 / 硬咆哮爵士 (Hard Bop)

1950s 中叶 · 蓝调与福音回潮 · 滚烫热烈之东岸律动
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. Moanin’ — Art Blakey & The Jazz Messengers Essential Track
  2. Song for My Father — Horace Silver
  3. The Sidewinder — Lee Morgan
Originating in the mid-1950s on the East Coast, hard bop was a soulful reinvigoration of bebop, reconnecting the music with raw African American blues and gospel roots. Driven by heavy-hitting bandleaders like Art Blakey and Horace Silver, hard bop offered driving swing, hummable melodies, and earthy rhythmic groove.
Listen on YouTube (Art Blakey & The Jazz Messengers - Moanin') Wikipedia Article

7. Key式爵士 (Modal Jazz)

1950s 末 · 摆脱频繁和弦桎梏 · 空灵写意与单调式即兴冥想
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. So What — Miles Davis Essential Track
  2. Flamenco Sketches — Miles Davis
  3. Impressions — John Coltrane
Pioneered in the late 1950s by Miles Davis and Bill Evans—immortalized in 1959's Kind of Blue—modal jazz abandoned rapid chord changes in favor of musical modes as the harmonic framework. This breakthrough gave improvisers unprecedented melodic freedom, spaciousness, and meditative emotional depth.
Listen on YouTube (Miles Davis - So What) Wikipedia Article

8. Jazz Fusion

1960s 末 ~ 1970s · 电子乐器革新 · 摇滚力量与复杂爵士和声共铸
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. Birdland — Weather Report Essential Track
  2. Chameleon — Herbie Hancock
  3. Birds of Fire — Mahavishnu Orchestra
Developed in the late 1960s and 1970s, jazz fusion combined jazz improvisation with the electric instruments, high volume, and driving funk/rock rhythms of the modern era. Led by Miles Davis's revolutionary Bitches Brew and iconic groups like Return to Forever and Weather Report, fusion expanded the sonic boundaries of jazz forever.
Listen on YouTube (Weather Report - Birdland) Wikipedia Article

专栏三：小流派与跨界爵士乐 (Sub-genres & Crossover Jazz)

1. 福音爵士与圣乐根源 (Gospel & Sacred Jazz)

非裔教堂灵歌 · 炽热击掌呼应与精神超拔
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. Come Sunday — Mahalia Jackson & Duke Ellington Essential Track
  2. Precious Lord, Take My Hand — Mahalia Jackson
  3. Oh Happy Day — Edwin Hawkins Singers
Rooted in the African American church, gospel music contributed emotional fervor, call-and-response vocal structures, and sacred harmonies directly into jazz. Masterpieces like Duke Ellington’s Sacred Concerts and Mahalia Jackson's sublime performance of "Come Sunday" revealed the profound spiritual bond between the two traditions.
Listen on YouTube (Mahalia Jackson & Duke Ellington - Come Sunday) Wikipedia Article
深度对比赏析：从欧洲民谣流行到黑人福音爵士的升华重构
经典案例：披头士原版《Yesterday》 vs. 科里·亨利（Cory Henry）钢琴“福音化（Gospel-ify）”重配版
【ENGLISH COMPARISON】
The Beatles' original 1965 live performance of "Yesterday" is an archetype of European folk-pop lyricism: Paul McCartney's acoustic guitar paired with a delicate classical string quartet creates an understated, melancholic masterpiece grounded in diatonic clarity. In this extraordinary KCRW live acoustic session, Grammy-winning pianist Cory Henry demonstrates the art of "gospel-ifying" a pop standard on acoustic grand piano. Stripping away the delicate restraint of the original, Henry infuses the melody with rich gospel voicings, dense chromatic reharmonizations, church blues runs, and impassioned dynamic builds. Comparing these two definitive takes vividly reveals how gospel harmony transforms a secular folk ballad into an ecstatic, soul-stirring spiritual journey.

2. 酸爵士 (Acid Jazz)

1980s 末英伦发端 · 黑胶采样 · 放克舞曲与灵魂乐摩登熔铸
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. Virtual Insanity — Jamiroquai Essential Track
  2. Still a Friend of Mine — Incognito
  3. Cantaloop (Flip Fantasia) — Us3
Originating in London clubs in the late 1980s, acid jazz fused rare-groove jazz records, soul, funk, and hip-hop beats with live instrumentation. Exemplified by acts like Jamiroquai, Incognito, and Us3, it introduced traditional jazz harmonies and brass motifs to dancefloors worldwide.
Listen on YouTube (Jamiroquai - Virtual Insanity) Wikipedia Article

3. 波萨诺瓦与拉丁爵士 (Bossa Nova & Latin Jazz)

1950s 末巴西海岸 · 桑巴摇曳微醺律动 · 印象派温润爵士和弦
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. The Girl from Ipanema — Stan Getz & Astrud Gilberto Essential Track
  2. Desafinado — João Gilberto
  3. Wave — Antônio Carlos Jobim
Born in late-1950s Brazil through the genius of Antônio Carlos Jobim and João Gilberto, Bossa Nova ("new trend") relaxed the feverish energy of samba into a cool, swaying acoustic guitar groove laced with complex jazz harmonies. Stan Getz's collaborations turned songs like "The Girl from Ipanema" into international anthems.
Listen on YouTube (The Girl From Ipanema) Wikipedia Article

4. 自由爵士与前卫先锋 (Free Jazz & Avant-Garde)

1960s · 抛弃预设和弦走向与规整节拍 · 纯粹声音探索与None界即兴
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. Lonely Woman — Ornette Coleman Essential Track
  2. Ascension — John Coltrane
  3. Ghosts — Albert Ayler
Spearheaded in the early 1960s by Ornette Coleman, Cecil Taylor, and late-period John Coltrane, free jazz dismantled established chord progressions, key centers, and regular meters. In doing so, it sought unfettered emotional expression and spontaneous collective communication beyond conventional harmonic boundaries.
Listen on YouTube (Ornette Coleman - Lonely Woman) Wikipedia Article

5. 平滑爵士 / 轻柔爵士 (Smooth Jazz)

1980s · 都会电台黄金风潮 · 优美舒缓旋律与温润声学质感
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. Just the Two of Us — Grover Washington Jr. Essential Track
  2. Breezin’ — George Benson
  3. Songbird — Kenny G
Gaining immense commercial popularity in the 1980s and '90s, smooth jazz combined pop production, polished R&B grooves, and accessible melodic hooks with jazz phrasing. While less complex than acoustic bebop, icons like Grover Washington Jr. and Kenny G introduced saxophone tones to millions across the globe.
Listen on YouTube (Grover Washington Jr. - Just the Two of Us) Wikipedia Article

专栏四：爵士乐影响 (Jazz Influence & Legacy)

1. 灵魂乐之催生 (Soul Music)

爵士管乐配器 · 布鲁斯音阶与福音情感的伟大交汇
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. Georgia On My Mind — Ray Charles Essential Track
  2. Respect — Aretha Franklin
  3. A Change Is Gonna Come — Sam Cooke
Soul music emerged in the 1950s and '60s when pioneers like Ray Charles fused African American gospel passion with jazz horn sections and blues changes. Jazz provided soul singers with nuanced vocal phrasing, complex chord substitutions, and swing feel, creating timeless anthems of love and civil rights.
Listen on YouTube (Ray Charles - Georgia On My Mind) Wikipedia Article

2. 节奏布鲁斯与当代 R&B / 新灵魂乐 (R&B & Neo-Soul)

大七与延伸和弦美学 · 自由声乐跑音与深沉后波普律动
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. Sir Duke — Stevie Wonder Essential Track
  2. Brown Sugar — D’Angelo
  3. On & On — Erykah Badu
From Motown legends to contemporary R&B and Neo-Soul, R&B owes its sophisticated harmonic language—such as maj7, 9th, and 11th chords—directly to jazz. Masterminds like Stevie Wonder, D'Angelo, and modern trailblazer Robert Glasper weave jazz virtuosity and phrasing directly into modern urban music.
Listen on YouTube (Stevie Wonder - Sir Duke) Wikipedia Article

3. 摇滚乐与前卫/数学摇滚 (Rock, Progressive & Math Rock)

Modal Improvisation · Odd Meters & Jazz Rock Epics
Starter Tracks · Essential Listening
  1. Aja — Steely Dan Essential Track
  2. Riders on the Storm — The Doors
  3. Shine On You Crazy Diamond — Pink Floyd
Rock music deepened dramatically when artists absorbed jazz principles. Jimi Hendrix studied modal improvisation, The Doors integrated cool jazz keyboards into psychedelic tracks, and Steely Dan crafted multi-platinum rock masterpieces built entirely on jazz chord extensions and premier jazz studio session players.

Standard Resolution · Down a 5th

Standard: ii - V7 - Imaj7

Dm7 → G7 → Cmaj7
  • Dominant Core: G7 contains 3rd (B) 与 ♭7th (F) forming a tense tritone interval.
  • Bassline: D → G → C (Down a Perfect 5th).
  • Voice Leading: B resolves up by half-step to C; F resolves down by half-step to E.
Tritone Sub · Chromatic Bass

Substitution: ii - ♭II7 - Imaj7

Dm7 → D♭7 → Cmaj7
  • Enharmonic Magic: D♭7 contains 3rd (F) 与 ♭7th (C♭/B) 恰好完全相同！仅角色互换！
  • Bassline: D → D♭ → C（极度丝滑的半音级进下行）。
  • 风格色彩： 波普（Bebop）与Cool Jazz最标志性的和声重新编配（Reharmonization）技巧。
核心原理解析： 什么是三全音（Tritone）？在十二平均律中，一个八度由 12 个半音组成，其正中间恰好是 6 个半音（三个全音，增四度或减五度）。因为两个相距三全音的属七和弦（例如 G7 与 D♭7）天然共享完全相同的两个骨干导音（3rd 与 ♭7th），因此可以None缝互换，不仅保持了同样强烈的解决冲动，还创造出了半音级进（D → D♭ → C）的迷人低音声部！

Column 6: Jazz Lore, Jargon & Standards Guide

JAZZ CULTURE & STANDARD SONGBOOK

Centennial Jazz Culture · Musician Jargon · Anecdotes & Standards Roadmap

Jazz is not just sophisticated theory—it is a living, humorous street culture. This section curates Jazz Jargon & Stage Etiquette, Legendary Master Trivia & Random Quotes, and a Tiered Jazz Standards Repertoire Roadmap.

The "Butter Notes" Epiphany
Literal: Butter Notes | Meaning: Skip the obvious chord tones
Miles Davis once leaned over to 23-year-old Herbie Hancock and whispered: “Don't play the butter notes.” Taking him literally, Hancock omitted the obvious 3rds and 7ths and stumbled upon modern suspended quartal voicings!
“Remove the most obvious notes, and true musical space is born.”
Woodshedding / Shed Practice
Literal: Woodshedding | Meaning: Intense solitary practice
Early musicians practiced in backyard woodsheds so they wouldn't disturb neighbors. Today, saying “I need to hit the shed” means locking yourself in to drill scales and transcriptions.
“Effortless grace on stage comes from a thousand hours in the woodshed.”
A "Clinker" (错音) Philosophy
Literal: Clinker | Meaning: A wrong note waiting to be resolved
As Thelonious Monk famously taught: there are no wrong notes in jazz—only where you resolve next. Slip a half-step into a chord tone, and a clinker turns into a brilliant chromatic approach!
“If you hit a wrong note, play it again with confidence—the audience will call it art.”
Trading Fours / Eights Stage
Literal: Trading 4s | Meaning: 4-bar call-and-response with drums
At the climax of solos, horn players and pianists trade 4-bar or 8-bar improvised phrases back and forth with the drummer—an electrifying musical conversation.
“Listen to the previous player's question and answer it in your four bars.”
The Head (乐曲头部) Form
Literal: The Head | Meaning: Main written melody of a tune
The classic jazz form is always: Head In (Main Melody) → Solos → Head Out (Return to Melody). A bandleader tapping their head on stage signals: “Bring it back to the melody!”
“When lost in chord changes, breathe and follow the Head home.”
Comping (切音伴奏) Comping
From Accompanying & Complementing
Syncopated chordal accompaniment by piano or guitar behind a soloist. Great comping never plods—it leaves breathing room and punctuates the soloist's phrases.
“What you leave out matters more than what you play; give the soloist air.”
Jam Session Stage Etiquette & Survival Guide
1. When to Applaud Don't wait until the end of the whole song! Applaud right after a musician finishes a great solo and steps back from the mic.
2. Tapping the Head If the bandleader taps the top of their head, it means “Back to the Head” to wrap up the tune—do not start another solo chorus!
3. Never Clap on 1 and 3 The soul of Swing lives on beats 2 and 4 (the backbeat)! Clapping on 1 and 3 kills the swing groove immediately.
4. Respect the Bass Solo When the bassist solos, drummers switch to quiet brushes and pianists lay out. Acoustic bass is subtle—keep the room quiet so everyone can hear.

Thelonious Monk
Top Hats & Spinning on Stage
Monk struck the keys with flat fingers like hammers and loved sharp dissonances. Mid-concert, he would often leave the piano bench to spin in circles dancing while horns soloed, then sit back down at the exact downbeat of the next chorus.
🎷
Charlie "Bird" Parker
The Plastic Saxophone & Fried Chicken
Nicknamed "Bird" for his 300 BPM bebop flights and love of chicken, Parker frequently pawned his horn before gigs. At the legendary 1953 Massey Hall concert, he performed on a borrowed plastic Grafton saxophone and delivered timeless magic.
🎺
Chet Baker
West Coast Whisper After Losing His Teeth
小号对嘴唇和牙齿的要求极高。1966 年切特·贝克在旧金山街头冲突中被打落门牙，几乎丧失吹奏能力。在加油站打工数年后，他装上假牙，硬是靠肌肉记忆重新摸索出了一种更贴近麦克风、气声迷离、脆弱深情的独特音色，反而铸就了晚年None可复制的传奇。
🎼
Bill Evans
Classical Impressionist in Jazz
埃文斯把德彪西和拉威尔的古典印象派色彩完全带入了爵士乐。当录制《Kind of Blue》contains《Blue in Green》时，戴维斯只写了两个和弦草稿，埃文斯却在五分钟内谱写出了如林间晨雾般的四度和声开篇。他是最孤独的诗人，弹琴时头低得几乎贴到琴键上。
⚡
Herbie Hancock
How a Wrong Note Became Genius
During a Stockholm concert, young Hancock played a terribly loud wrong chord behind Miles Davis. Mortified, Hancock froze—until Miles immediately played a blistering phrase that made that "wrong" chord sound completely intentional!
🎻
Charles Mingus
Fury, Bass & Deep Tenderness
Known as jazz's angriest man, Mingus once smashed his own bass when club audiences talked over his music. Yet he was equally tender, composing the heartbreaking elegy Goodbye Pork Pie Hat for Lester Young.
